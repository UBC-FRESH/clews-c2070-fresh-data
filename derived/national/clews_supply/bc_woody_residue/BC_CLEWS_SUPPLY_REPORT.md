# All-BC woody-residue supply curve for OSeCAN zones B1/B2

Status: **FRESH-side provisional**. This is a derived supply curve for the
CLEWs-C2070 biomass seam. It is not an official C2070/OSeCAN boundary, and it
is not a production-calibrated fuel offer.

Generated by `workflows/national/clews/bc_residue_supply.py` from the derived
artifacts listed in `manifest.json`. The companion machine-readable tables are
in this directory; the audit tables are under `audit/` and the comparison
scenarios under `comparison/`.

### Why AAC is primary

The C2070 horizon is 2021–2070. A static 2018 apportionment is a **tenure
snapshot**, not a sustainable annual harvest level. The apportionment workbook
includes MPB-salvage and non-replaceable volumes that are above the AAC in
Prince George (11.019M m³ apportionment versus 8.375M m³ AAC) and in several
other TSAs. Carrying that 2018 snapshot flat for 50 years would overstate
long-run residue supply. The 2018 AAC is the sustainable annual cut and is the
better static basis for a 2021–2070 curve.

The parsed 36-TSA AAC artifact is complete for the TSAs it contains, but it
omits Prince George because the workbook `PG` sheet has only eight year columns
(the generic parser requires ten). The primary therefore adds the Prince George
2018 AAC of **8,375,000 m³/y** from the workbook `Region 2` tab, giving
**52,774,801 m³/y** across all 37 active TSAs.

The parsed apportionment artifact assigns **zero** to four TSAs, not just a
missing Prince George: Dawson Creek, Fort Nelson, Fort St. John, and Okanagan
use an `Indicated Coniferous Apportionment` header (Okanagan uses a backtick),
so the parser finds no apportionment block; Prince George is absent because of
the year-column problem. The apportionment comparison adds their 2018 class
sums from the workbook:

| TSA | 2018 apportionment (m³/y) | 2018 AAC (m³/y) | Evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Dawson Creek | 1,860,000 | 1,860,000 | `Indicated Coniferous Apportionment` class sum |
| Fort Nelson | 1,625,000 | 1,625,000 | `Indicated Coniferous Apportionment` class sum |
| Fort St. John | 2,115,010 | 2,115,000 | `Indicated Coniferous Apportionment` class sum |
| Okanagan | 3,078,406 | 3,078,406 | apportionment block with a backtick header |
| Prince George | 11,019,167.5 | 8,375,000 | `PG` 2018 class sum; `Region 2` AAC |

The apportionment comparison is materially higher than the AAC (58.424M versus
52.775M, +10.7%). It is reported because the task asked for the AAC versus
apportionment comparison, but it is **not** the primary basis.

### Profile

The annual profile is **flat**: the same 2018 value in every year 2021–2070.
The `basis` column is `static_aac`; the `volume_basis` column is
`aac_2018_all_bc`; the `profile` column is `flat`. No time path, climate
scenario, MPB trajectory, or future AAC reduction is invented.

The residual 2018 MPB uplift in the AAC is already embedded in the AAC values
for the TSAs that carry it. A future AAC decline is not modelled.

## 4. Residue ratio

### Batch ratio (chosen)

The area-weighted national batch join supplies a log stream and a residue
stream by ecoprovince:

- BC batch log volume: **8,936,253,982 m³**.
- BC batch residue: **722,691,931 ODT**.
- BC all-species ratio: **0.080872 ODT/m³**.
- BC-relevant batch cells: 429; 192 matched, 237 `no_area` (zero totals, no
  1 ha placeholder).

The batch is the BC area-weighted application of the national representative-
stand bucking matrix. Its residue stream is **taper residue** (the
non-merchantable stem/top portion after bucking), not full aboveground slash
(branches, bark, foliage). That is a deliberate and conservative definition:
it matches the "woody residue" stream the WS3/C2070 seam is meant to carry.

The ecoprovince ratios differ by an order of magnitude, because species and
taper geometry differ:

| Ecoprovince | Zone | Residue ratio (ODT/m³) |
|---|---|---:|
| Georgia Depression | B1 | 0.035732 |
| Southern Coastal Mountains | B1 | 0.036364 |
| Columbia Montane Cordillera | B2 | 0.044772 |
| Southern Montane Cordillera | B2 | 0.054774 |
| Northern Montane Cordillera | B2 | 0.091144 |
| Northern Coastal Mountains | B2 | 0.108105 |
| Central Montane Cordillera | B2 | 0.138398 |
| Central Boreal Plains | B2 | 0.141327 |
| Boreal Foothills | B2 | 0.173591 |
| Hay-Slave Lowlands | B2 | 0.193776 |
| Northern Boreal Cordillera | B2 | 0.225638 |
| Southern Boreal Cordillera | B2 | 0.364484 |
| Wrangel Mountains | B2 | 0.216751 |

The primary applies the **ecoprovince** ratio to the ecoprovince share of
harvest. The all-species ratio is retained as a comparison: applying 0.080872
to every zone would move about 7.6 PJ from B2 to B1 and change the national
total from 93.170 PJ to 78.958 PJ. The ecoprovince resolution is more
defensible for a zonal supply curve because the coastal and boreal ratios are
not interchangeable.

`NO_ECOPROVINCE` has no batch ratio; the 55 m³ allocated to it uses the BC
all-species fallback and is flagged. `Wrangel Mountains` has a batch ratio but
no THLB, so it receives no harvest volume.

### Stage B ratio (reported, not used)

The TSA24/BC Stage B pilot generated **8,325,597.703 ODT** of mill residue from
**32,076,713.233 m³** of sawmill log input: **0.259553 ODT/m³**.

It is not used as the primary ratio:

1. It is a **mill-processing** ratio (chips, sawdust/shavings, hogfuel/bark,
   other), not a harvest-residue ratio. Applying it to the harvest volume
   would double count the log input that generated the mill residue.
2. It is a total generated-mill-residue ratio. Most of that residue is already
   consumed by pulp, pellet, and other mills; the Stage B surplus available to
   BIO is only **1,824,158 ODT** (0.056869 ODT/m³ of sawmill input), and the
   hogfuel-only BIO allocation is 842,498 ODT.
3. The Stage B pilot is a diagnostic allocation with 43 of 94 sawmills on
   TL-hub proxy coordinates, not a BC-wide residue census.

The Stage B total ratio and the unassigned ratio are both reported in
`audit/bc_residue_ratio_comparison.csv` for audit.

## 5. ODT → PJ conversion

- **ODT** = oven-dry tonne (zero moisture).
- **LHV** = 18.5 GJ/ODT on a dry basis. This is a conservative mid-range value
  for woody biomass; the exact species mix would move it by roughly ±1 GJ/ODT.
- **1 PJ = 1,000,000 GJ**.
- `residue_pj = residue_odt × 18.5 / 1,000,000`.

No moisture credit is applied. If the delivered fuel is at 45% wet basis, the
as-received energy per wet tonne is lower, but the OSeCAN cap is an energy cap
on the dry residue mass; the moisture assumption belongs to the power-plant
conversion technology, not to this residue ledger. The Stage B density of
0.45 ODT/m³ green is used only to convert transport costs, not to convert the
residue cap.

supply curve, not to the residue ledger.

## 8. Cost basis and derivation

The cost is derived from the TSA24/BC Stage B pilot, not from the C2070
placeholder:

1. Take the 94 Stage B sawmill sources and their generated residue ODT
   (`stage_b/residue_supply.csv`).
2. For each source, take the **least-cost road link to any BIO recipient**
   (`stage_b/stage_b_transport_links.csv`), in CAD/m³ green.
3. Convert to CAD/ODT with the source density (0.45 ODT/m³).
4. Add the BIO recipient operating cost (`residue_demand.csv`,
   20.890031 CAD/ODT) as the fuel preparation/handling proxy.
5. Map each sawmill to a TSA (`bc_facility_tsa.csv`) and then to B1/B2 via the
   TSA crosswalk.
6. Weight by generated residue ODT within each zone.
7. Divide by 18.5 GJ/ODT to get $/GJ.

| Zone | Transport (CAD/ODT) | Processing/handling (CAD/ODT) | Total (CAD/ODT) | $/GJ | Sawmill sources | ODT weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B1 | 34.509592 | 20.890031 | 55.399623 | 2.994574 | 28 | 1,130,673 |
| B2 | 10.683102 | 20.890031 | 31.573133 | 1.706656 | 66 | 7,194,925 |
| CAN | 12.258924 | 20.890031 | 33.148955 | 1.791835 | 94 | 5,036,221 (cap weight) |

The B1 cost is high because the coastal road network forces long hauls (the
least-cost BIO link is still 34.51 CAD/ODT before processing). The B2 cost is
low because most Interior sawmills are close to a BIO plant (least-cost link
median about 142 km). The national row is the **PJ-cap-weighted** average of
B1 and B2, so it reconciles with the national cap; it is not the raw Stage B
source-weighted average (1.881564 $/GJ), which is also reported in the audit.

### Cost caveats

- The cost is a **mill-residue delivered-cost proxy**. It excludes
  stump-to-roadside collection, bundling, grinding, and any stumpage or
  opportunity cost for harvest slash. It is a **lower bound** for a
  harvest-residue supply curve.
- The Stage B transport links use the NRN BC 2018 road graph only. No rail,
  water, or ferry route is assumed. Coastal B1 is therefore penalised.
- 43 of 94 sawmills carry TL-hub proxy coordinates; their link distances are
  low-confidence. The least-cost envelope reduces, but does not remove, that
  bias.
- Stage B costs are **CAD**, nominally 2026-era FFCM prices. The OSeCAN
  `VariableCost` is `$2020`. No deflator or exchange rate is available in the
  repo, so the values are carried as CAD 2020-equivalent and flagged. A 10–15%
  deflator would change them by less than the cost uncertainty.
- The BIO operating cost (20.890031 CAD/ODT) is the FFCM processing-line cost;
  it is used as a fuel preparation/handling proxy, not as a measured
  harvest-residue collection cost.

## 10. Caveats and gaps

1. **No published C2070 boundary.** B1/B2 is a FRESH-side proposal. Any C2070
   team using these rows must accept or replace the crosswalk.
2. **THLB-weighted TSA split.** Harvest is assumed uniform per THLB hectare
   within a TSA. The area weights are VRI 2025 polygons; the TSA/ecoregion
   labels come from a 500 m raster, not exact polygon assignment.
3. **Taper residue, not full slash.** The batch residue stream is the
   non-merchantable stem/top portion. It excludes branches, bark, and foliage.
   The AGB-based harvest-slash ledger is larger (about 0.48 ODT/m³ gross,
   0.29 ODT/m³ recoverable at 60%). Using it would raise the cap roughly 3×.
4. **No recovery factor.** The primary cap is gross residue, not the
   economically or operationally recoverable fraction. The 60% recovery
   sensitivity gives 55.902 PJ national.
5. **Static 2018 basis.** No future AAC decline, MPB trajectory, climate
   change, wildfire, or disturbance is modelled. The profile is flat.
6. **Parser gaps.** The generic TSA apportionment parser misses Prince George
   (eight year columns) and Dawson Creek/Fort Nelson/Fort St. John/Okanagan
   (`Indicated Coniferous Apportionment` or backtick headers). The primary AAC
   only needs the Prince George correction; the apportionment comparison adds
   all five. The corrections are derived from the workbook and listed in
   `audit/bc_apportionment_adjustments.csv`.
7. **PG apportionment inconsistency.** The PG sheet's 2018 apportionment class
   sum (11,019,167.5 m³) is above its 2018 AAC (8,375,000 m³) and 0.3% above
   the `Region 2` total minus Robson (10,982,726.5 m³). The primary avoids this
   by using AAC; the apportionment comparison carries the inconsistency.
8. **Mill-residue cost proxy.** The cost is not a harvest-slash collection
   cost. It is a lower bound for harvest residue and should be replaced when a
   documented stump-to-roadside collection/grinding cost is available.
9. **Currency vintage.** Stage B costs are CAD 2026-era; OSeCAN is $2020. No
   deflator is applied.
10. **NO_ECOPROVINCE and Wrangel.** `NO_ECOPROVINCE` is a 2,337 ha fallback;
    Wrangel has no THLB. Both are B2 but contribute essentially nothing.
11. **National rollup is not additive.** `SUPBCW` is the sum of B1 and B2 for
    comparison only. Do not load it with the zone technologies.

## 12. Bottom line

For OSeCAN, the defensible FRESH-side provisional rows are:

- `SUPBCWB1`: **6.162027 PJ/yr** at **2.994574 $/GJ** (B1).
- `SUPBCWB2`: **87.008058 PJ/yr** at **1.706656 $/GJ** (B2).
- National comparison: **93.170084 PJ/yr** at **1.791835 $/GJ**.

The current 50 PJ/$4 placeholder is conservative on both quantity and cost for
this gross taper-residue definition. The largest unresolved uncertainty is not
the ratio or the LHV; it is the **recoverable fraction** of harvest residue and
the **missing collection/grinding cost**. Both should be resolved before these
rows are treated as a production supply curve.
